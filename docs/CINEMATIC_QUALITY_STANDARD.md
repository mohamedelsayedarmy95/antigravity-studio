# Cinematic Quality Standard

Review each aspect at native size and at its intended viewing size. Watch first without sound for clarity of the story, then listen against picture for timing and balance. Record timecodes, aspect, issue, and requested correction against the exact reviewed master.

| Review area | What the reviewer checks |
| --- | --- |
| Narrative and editorial | The opening establishes a reason to watch. Each beat advances the idea, shows evidence, or makes the next action clear. The ending resolves the proposition. |
| Product fidelity | Screen content, gestures, state changes, and result copy match the product. Crop and overlays preserve details needed to understand the demonstration. |
| Camera, light, and depth | Moves reveal hierarchy, geography, or a result. Lighting and reflections make materials believable. 2D and spatial treatments support the brand and remain legible through transitions. |
| Motion and typography | Cuts, entrances, settles, and exits create a readable rhythm. Text has hierarchy, safe margins, and adequate hold time. Review wrapping, joining scripts, mixed directions, and numerals with a fluent reader when applicable. |
| Aspect and mobile composition | Reframe the focal point, product UI, and type for each requested ratio. Inspect on a phone for readability, overlap, and platform UI intrusions. |
| Sound | Listen for intelligibility, appropriate dynamics, clean starts and ends, cue-to-picture sync, and brand fit. Narration and accessibility follow project requirements. |

A contact sheet helps find framing issues. Watch the whole edit to judge motion, pacing, cuts, and sound. Record the visual review separately from the owner or director's final creative decision.
